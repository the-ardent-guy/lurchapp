MARK RATIONALE
THE MARK'S ORIGIN
Derived, Not Designed
The surya mark is not a logo invented for Bijouri. It is the sunburst ring from the Bijouri range — traced to its geometric proportions and reproduced as a two-dimensional form. The mark and the product are the same object at different scales. This is what makes it irreplaceable: it cannot be separated from what Bijouri actually makes.
THUNDER CONNECTION
Force From a Single Point
Thunder radiates. The shockwave moves outward from a single point of discharge in all directions simultaneously. The surya mark is this exact geometry — a central origin, force moving outward in sixteen directions. The garnet at the centre is the point of discharge. The spokes are the force already in motion. The mark embodies the brand name in its structure.
WHAT EVOLIND BRINGS
Weight Without Heaviness
Evolind carries more presence than a typical refined serif — the letterforms are wider, the serifs more emphatic, the overall feel closer to a typeface that has earned its place rather than been refined to near-invisibility. Against the delicate radial mark, the stronger wordmark creates the right tension: the mark is fine and precise, the name is solid and present.
THE GARNET RULE
One Colour, One Location
The garnet accent (#8B1A1A) appears in exactly one place: the centre of the surya mark. Nowhere else in the primary identity system. It is the gem at the origin of the force. In physical applications it appears as a wax seal in garnet-coloured wax, the single ribbon accent, or one rule element on the care card. The constraint is the rule. The constraint is also the power.
KARIGARI-DERIVED ELEMENTS
The Pattern Vocabulary
Every element in the Bijouri visual system is sourced from techniques present in the product range. Not borrowed from Indian decoration generally — extracted from Bijouri's specific making vocabulary.
Radiating Surya Form
Repoussé / Cast
The primary brand mark. Derived from the sunburst ring in the product range — oxidised silver spokes radiating from a citrine centre. The surya (sun) form appears in Rajasthani karigari as both a repoussé motif and a cast form. In Bijouri's range it is the most visually precise combination of silver surface work and gemstone setting. The mark is the product at a different scale.
APPLICATION
Primary mark · All applications · All scales
Cartouche with Arabesque
Nakashi Scrollwork
The secondary seal mark. In the Bijouri range, the nakashi cartouche appears as a shaped four-lobed silver pendant with hand-engraved scrollwork arabesques inside. The boundary of the cartouche is organic-geometric — neither a circle nor a square, but a contained form derived from repeating circles in the Rajasthani craft tradition. Used as the brand's formal seal on care cards, wax stamps, and correspondence.
APPLICATION
Seal mark · Care cards · Wax stamps · Formal print
Tarkashi Lattice
Filigree Wirework
The surface texture element. Tarkashi is the Jaipur tradition of filigree wirework — fine silver wires twisted and soldered into open lace-like forms. The lattice pattern appears in the earring tops and drop earrings in the Bijouri range, where individual wire cells are visible at close range. As a brand element, the tarkashi lattice is used exclusively as a surface texture — felt before it is seen, as a blind emboss on packaging surfaces.
APPLICATION
Surface texture only · Blind emboss on packaging · Never foreground
TYPOGRAPHY
Three Fonts. Three Roles.
No exceptions.
WORDMARK ONLY
Evolind
Evolind carries the wordmark and nothing else. Its inscription-derived letterforms — wider serifs, emphatic thick-thin contrast — give BIJOURI authority without decoration. It stands alone. The surya mark is its companion when appropriate, not its crutch. Never bold. Never italic. Never used for headlines, body, or any function other than the wordmark.
WORDMARK SPECIMEN
DISPLAY HEADINGS · BODY COPY · EDITORIAL
EB Garamond
EB Garamond carries everything the brand says — display headlines, body stories, product descriptions, karigar narratives. The 16th century Garamond punchcuts are genuinely evergreen. The thin-thick contrast in Regular weight mirrors nakashi pressure variation: thick downstrokes, hairline cross-strokes — the font looks carved because it is descended from the same hand-tool logic.

NAVIGATION · PRICING · LABELS · SPECS
Neue Haas Grotesk
Neue Haas Grotesk carries all functional information — pricing, karigar credit lines, navigation links, specifications, sub-lines, care instructions. It is the humanist sans from which Helvetica was derived, but warmer in its letterforms. Regular weight only. It never draws attention to itself. It gets out of the way.

TONE OF VOICE
How Bijouri Speaks
Precise, not cold.
Every word is chosen. Warmth comes from specificity — the karigar's name, the technique, the exact material. Not from warmth-performance.
Grounded, not earnest.
The brand does not need you to understand it. It simply is what it is. No explanations. No apologising for its origin or its ambition.
Intelligent, not clever.
The intelligence is in the making. The communication reflects this — it is not trying to be interesting. It is interesting because it is specific.

BRAND STANDARDS
Correct & Incorrect Usage
DO
—
Derive every visual element from the product range
—
Name the karigar on every touchpoint
—
State '925 Sterling Silver' specifically, always
—
Show surface texture in all product photography
—
Use EB Garamond for all editorial headings and body
—
Keep garnet to the mark centre only
—
Trust the charcoal ground — it is correct
—
Show the workshop as it actually is, not romanticised
—
Let the object be the hero — always
—
Call it patina, never tarnish
—
Give the wordmark generous clear space
—
Use directional light that reveals surface texture
DON'T
×
Use gold tones anywhere in the system, ever
×
Use marble, crystals, or flowers as props
×
Write 'handcrafted with love' or 'artisan-made'
×
Use bold weight on the BIJOURI wordmark
×
Use Evolind for headlines or body copy
×
Use garnet as a full background colour
×
Use ring lights — they destroy surface texture
×
Add drop shadows or gradients to any brand element
×
Use generic Indian decoration patterns
×
Show lifestyle images where jewellery is secondary
×
Set the wordmark tighter than +140 tracking
×
Use a second typeface not in the three-font system
